### **The Visual Layout (The Visual Collision)**

This page should use a vertical split or an asymmetric side-by-side design to establish a stark, jarring aesthetic contrast:

- **Left Side: The Crown (The Symmetrical Vacuum)**
    - **The Look:** Cold, sterile, desaturated wide-shots of Buckingham Palace corridors and Ministry of Defence briefing rooms.
    - **The Rule:** Absolute Wes Anderson-style symmetry. The architecture is massive, grand, and frozen in time. The human beings inside these frames are dwarfed by centuries of inherited stone, looking isolated, rigid, and deeply paranoid.
- **Right Side: The Band (The Cluttered Canvas)**
    - **The Look:** Warm, high-contrast, amber-tinted close-ups of Abbey Road Studio Two.
    - **The Rule:** Cluttered, living, and claustrophobic. The frame is packed with cigarette smoke, tangled microphone cables, half-empty teacups, lyric sheets scribbled on napkins, and glowing amplifier tubes. The camera here breathes, moves, and feels fluid, capturing a volcanic creative explosion happening in real-time.

### **THE WORLD**

*Her Majesty’s Displeasure* is set in the shifting fault lines of late-1960s London—a city actively tearing itself away from its imperial past. The series functions as a claustrophobic, dark workplace comedy split between two highly insulated, opposing bubbles: the rigid, silent, administrative halls of British power and the loud, chaotic, smoke-filled incubator of Abbey Road Studio Two.

### **THE MACRO TIMELINE: FROM MEDALS TO RADICALISM**

The five-year narrative arc tracks the fastest, most volatile cultural transformation in modern British history:

- **1965 (The Polite Mirage):** The era of the clean-cut "mop-tops." The band wears matching tailored collarless suits, accepts royal honors, and plays by the establishment's rules. The Crown believes they can be managed with subtle, polite bureaucratic "nudges."
- **1968–1970 (The Raw Awakening):** The illusion breaks. As the global anti-war movement ignites and John Lennon radicalizes, the band grows long hair, embraces avant-garde chaos, and publicly throws their royal honors back at the palace gates.

### **THE SATIRICAL CORE**

As the counter-culture accelerates completely out of the state's control, Prince Philip's intelligence operation spirals from a petty exercise in domestic surveillance into a desperate, panicked thriller. The show captures the exact moment the British Empire realizes its grand, centuries-old machinery of control is entirely powerless against four working-class boys armed with feedback loops, Eastern philosophy, and a guitar amplifier.

When you deliver this page, you are telling the buyer: **"This is not a static period piece."** You are showing them that the wardrobe, the music, the colors, and the camera movement itself will dramatically shift across the six episodes. It promises a highly dynamic visual journey where the sterile, grey palette of MI5 is gradually infected and overwhelmed by the psychedelic, raw, counter-culture energy of the late '60s.
